## 1. Purpose and scope

This procedure covers the use of hydrofluoric acid (HF) at concentrations above 1 percent in the teaching and research laboratories of the department. It applies to everyone who handles HF, including visiting researchers. Work with HF outside a fume hood is not covered by this procedure and is not permitted.

## 2. Hazards

HF is corrosive and, unlike other acids, penetrates skin and binds calcium in tissue and bone. Burns from dilute solutions can be painless for several hours, by which time serious damage has occurred. Exposure of 2 percent of body surface to concentrated HF can be fatal. Inhalation of vapour damages the lungs.

## 3. Responsibilities

The laboratory manager maintains the stock of calcium gluconate gel, checks it monthly and replaces it before its expiry date. Principal investigators ensure that everyone in their group has completed HF training before first use. The departmental safety officer audits HF work areas each semester and keeps the training records.

## 4. Procedure

1. Confirm that the fume hood's face velocity reads at least 0.5 m/s on the monitor before starting. Do not use a hood whose monitor shows a fault.
2. Put on a lab coat, a face shield over safety goggles, and HF-rated gloves (neoprene or butyl, not nitrile). Check the gloves for pinholes by inflating them.
3. Place the calcium gluconate gel within arm's reach inside the work area and check that it is in date.
4. Work only with the volume you need for the step; never return unused HF to the stock bottle.
5. Neutralise waste with calcium carbonate in the hood, label the container "HF waste, neutralised" and move it to the satellite accumulation area the same day.
6. Wash gloved hands before removing gloves, then wash hands again with soap for one minute.

## 5. Emergency

If HF touches skin: flush with water for five minutes, then apply calcium gluconate gel to the area and keep massaging it in while someone calls campus emergency on 8-2121 and says "hydrofluoric acid exposure". Do not stop the gel for a painless burn. Take the safety data sheet to the hospital.
