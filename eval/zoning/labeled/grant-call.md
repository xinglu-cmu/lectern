# Call for Proposals: Seed Grants in Computational Sustainability 2027

Office of Research Development · Issued 1 October 2026

## Purpose

The seed grant programme funds early-stage projects that apply computational methods to sustainability problems, with the aim of producing preliminary results that make the project competitive for external funding. Up to eight awards of $40,000 each will be made for projects starting in January 2027.

## Eligibility

Principal investigators must hold a faculty or research-track appointment at the university. Graduate students and postdoctoral researchers may be named as co-investigators but may not lead a proposal. A principal investigator may submit one proposal per cycle.

## What to submit

Proposals must include:

1. A project description of no more than three pages, covering the problem, the approach, and the preliminary result the seed funding will produce.
2. A budget and a half-page justification.
3. A two-page CV for each investigator.
4. A data management statement of no more than half a page.

Submit a single PDF through the research portal by 5 pm on 14 November 2026. Late or incomplete proposals will not be reviewed. Questions may be sent to seedgrants@example.edu until 7 November.

## Review criteria

Proposals are scored on significance of the sustainability problem (30%), soundness of the computational approach (30%), likelihood that the preliminary result leads to external funding (25%), and feasibility within the budget and timeline (15%).

## Example of a competitive scope

A six-month project that assembles a labelled dataset of satellite images for a specific land-use question, trains a baseline model, and reports accuracy against the current manual process, positioned as the pilot for a federal proposal.

## Use of AI tools in proposals

Applicants may use AI writing tools to draft text, but the project description must reflect the applicants' own research plan, and any substantive use of AI tools must be disclosed in a note at the end of the description. Reviewers are instructed not to use AI tools to evaluate proposals.
