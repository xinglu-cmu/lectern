# Lab 3 Report: Measuring g with a Simple Pendulum

Physics 101 · Section B · Submitted 29 September 2026 · Partner: J. Okafor

## Objective

To measure the local acceleration due to gravity using a simple pendulum and to compare the result with the accepted value of 9.81 m/s².

## Theory

For small angles, the period of a simple pendulum depends only on its length and on g: T = 2π√(L/g). Measuring the period for several lengths and plotting T² against L gives a straight line whose slope is 4π²/g.

## Method

We used a steel bob on a thread, measured from the pivot to the centre of the bob with a metre rule, and timed twenty oscillations with a stopwatch for each of six lengths between 0.30 m and 0.80 m. Each timing was repeated three times. The release angle was kept below 10°.

## Results

| L (m) | T (s) | T² (s²) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.30 | 1.10 | 1.21 |
| 0.40 | 1.27 | 1.61 |
| 0.50 | 1.42 | 2.02 |
| 0.60 | 1.56 | 2.43 |
| 0.70 | 1.68 | 2.82 |
| 0.80 | 1.80 | 3.24 |

The slope of T² against L was 4.04 s²/m, giving g = 9.77 m/s².

## Discussion

Our value is 0.4% below the accepted value, within our estimated uncertainty of 1.5%, which is dominated by reaction time on the stopwatch. Timing twenty oscillations rather than one reduced that contribution; a photogate would reduce it further. The small-angle approximation introduces an error below 0.2% at 10°.

## Conclusion

The pendulum method gives g = 9.77 ± 0.15 m/s², consistent with the accepted value.
